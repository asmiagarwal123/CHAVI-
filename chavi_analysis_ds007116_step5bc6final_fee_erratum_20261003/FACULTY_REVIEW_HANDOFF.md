## What this work asks

The narrow research question is whether a bootstrap that holds an estimated cross-fitted reference transformation fixed (P2) behaves differently from procedures that resample and refit it (P3/P4), when longitudinal five-field profiles are summarized by level ICC, mean level change, and RMS centered-shape displacement. The bounded contribution is failure-aware reporting: conditional coverage is reported alongside interval availability and available-and-covered probability.

The repaired simulations establish that these procedures can differ under the prespecified mechanisms. In continuous S1, P3 improved RMS conditional coverage relative to P2 but was still below 0.95. In rounded/tied S5, P3 had 0.956349 conditional RMS coverage among available intervals but only 1,260/2,000 intervals were available, giving 0.6025 available-and-covered probability. The 52-pair Penn LEAD analysis is an empirical sensitivity illustration of frozen estimates and intervals; it does not establish development, clinical change, task equivalence, causal effects, or individual-profile reliability.

Measurement metadata remain incomplete: the sources do not establish all LNB denominators/form composition, CTAP physical units/exact aggregation, or a task-version equivalence crosswalk. Conditional coverage and availability answer different questions and should not be collapsed.
