# Final scope and research question

## Consolidated scope

This study evaluates how uncertainty and resampling failure behave when longitudinal summaries of five reference-scaled cognitive/motor score fields depend on an estimated reference transformation. It then illustrates the prespecified summaries and sensitivity analyses in Penn LEAD/ds007116 aggregates.

## Primary research question

For two-occasion, five-task profiles transformed using a cross-fitted reference sample, how do a fixed-transformation participant bootstrap (P2) and a joint participant bootstrap that refits the transformation (P3) differ in interval coverage, availability, and width under prespecified continuous and tied/discrete simulations, and what do the corresponding empirical estimates show under frozen primary and sensitivity specifications?

## Objectives

1. Decompose profiles into level and centered shape and calculate the frozen ICC(A,1), mean level change, and pooled RMS shape displacement.
2. Evaluate P1–P4, MAD/SD, percentile/basic intervals across the repaired six-scenario pilot, while reserving confirmatory claims for S1/S5.
3. Report coverage conditional on availability, interval availability, available-and-covered proportion, width, failures, and Monte Carlo uncertainty together.
4. Apply P2 and P3 to the frozen 52-pair primary analysis and report prespecified sensitivities without causal attribution.

## Supported claims

- Reference-estimation uncertainty can materially alter interval behavior for some endpoint/mechanism combinations.
- Failure-aware reporting changes interpretation: high conditional coverage may coexist with substantial interval unavailability.
- In S1/MAD RMS percentile results, P3 coverage exceeds P2 but remains below 0.95.
- In S5/MAD RMS percentile results, P3 has substantial unavailability; P2 has near-complete availability.
- The empirical P2 and P3 RMS intervals differ more in realized width than the level endpoints, descriptively.
- Empirical conclusions vary across scale, support/model, eligibility, and reselection sensitivities.

## Exclusions

No diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, normative-population inference, causal developmental effect, pure practice-effect estimate, measurement-equivalence claim, or proof of individual-profile reliability is supported. The project does not claim invention of cross-fitting, MAD scaling, bootstrap refitting, ICC, profile decomposition, or simulation-reporting principles. Pilot scenarios S2–S4/S6 are exploratory stress tests, not confirmatory results.

## Remaining hypotheses

Whether the real task-score distributions and reference fits are sufficiently represented by S1, S5, or another mechanism remains untested. Whether task versions are measurement-equivalent, how letter n-back load contributes to its recorded score, and the precise finger-tapping unit/aggregation remain unresolved. These are metadata/measurement questions, not permission to retrofit new estimands.
