# Abstract and contribution statement

## Working title

Failure aware bootstrap evaluation for reference scaled longitudinal five field profiles with a Penn LEAD illustration

## Structured abstract

**Background:** Longitudinal multivariate profiles are often standardized against an estimated reference sample, but routine participant bootstraps may hold that transformation fixed. The consequences for interval performance and resampling failure are rarely reported together.

**Methods:** We studied three prespecified summaries of two-occasion, five-field profiles: level ICC(A,1), mean level change, and pooled RMS centered-shape displacement. A cross-fitted Design B transformation excluded each target from its own reference-fold fit. Repaired simulations compared an oracle conditional method (P1), fixed-transformation bootstrap (P2), joint unstratified refitting bootstrap (P3), and fold-stratified refitting bootstrap (P4), with MAD/SD scales and percentile/basic intervals. The six-scenario 100-replicate pilot was exploratory; only continuous S1 and rounded/tied S5 were confirmatory (2,000 outer replicates). We report conditional coverage, availability, available-and-covered probability, width, failures, and stored paired Monte Carlo uncertainty. A frozen Penn LEAD/ds007116 analysis illustrates the workflow.

**Results:** In confirmatory S1 MAD-scale RMS percentile intervals, P2 coverage was 0.803 (1,606/2,000) and P3 coverage was 0.9175 (1,835/2,000), with full availability; P3 remained below 0.95. In S5, P2 availability was 0.997 and available-and-covered probability was 0.916, whereas P3 availability was 0.630 and available-and-covered probability was 0.6025. In the 52-pair primary empirical analysis, ICC(A,1) was 0.619, mean level change was 0.366 reference-scaled units, and RMS shape displacement was 1.002 reference-scaled units. The P3 percentile RMS interval was 47.4% wider than P2 (a realized descriptive comparison).

**Conclusions:** Joint refitting can change interval performance but does not deliver universal nominal calibration and may incur material unavailability with tied/discrete-like data. A defensible evaluation therefore reports coverage and availability jointly. The Penn LEAD analysis is a worked sensitivity illustration, not evidence of causal development, individual-profile reliability, or task-version equivalence.

## Contribution statement

The contribution is a linked, reproducible, failure-aware evaluation of fixed versus jointly refitted estimated reference transformations for longitudinal five-field profile summaries. Cross-fitting, robust scaling, ICC, profile decomposition, bootstrap resampling, and ADEMP reporting are established antecedents. The paper contributes their prespecified integration, repaired simulation evidence, explicit availability/failure accounting, and an independently validated aggregate empirical illustration.
