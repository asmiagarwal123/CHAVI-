# Journal-fit comparison (checked 2026-10-03)

## Provisional route: PLOS ONE

PLOS ONE explicitly considers research articles and research methods, judges technical soundness and whether conclusions are supported, and permits preprints. Its author guidance requires data underlying figures/statistics and expects author-generated analytical code to be freely available, normally with an enduring repository identifier. This manuscript's cautious, methods-and-simulation focus fits that editorial model, but it cannot be submitted on the current package alone: authors must complete an authorized public code/aggregate-data release plan and journal-specific formatting before submission.

Official records: <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/submission-guidelines>, <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/data-availability>, <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/code-availability>.

## Alternative: BMJ Open

BMJ Open considers secondary database analyses, modelling studies, pilot studies, and methods work that directly addresses patient outcomes or healthcare practice. It requires a data-availability statement for research articles, requests figures as separate files, uses open peer review, and lists an APC. The CHAVI paper's non-clinical cognitive-methods emphasis may make topical fit less direct than PLOS ONE; authors should ask the editorial office if unsure.

Official records: <https://bmjopen.bmj.com/pages/research-aims-and-scope>, <https://bmjopen.bmj.com/pages/authors>.

## Alternative: specialist-methods journal (route to be confirmed)

A specialist statistical or psychometric journal may be attractive if faculty decide the methodological contribution, rather than the Penn LEAD illustration, should dominate. No recommendation is made here because a specific journal's current scope, article category, data/code policy, fees, and formatting requirements have not been verified for this package.

## Decision

Provisional faculty recommendation: develop the PLOS ONE route only after an authorized archival plan for code and permitted aggregate data is agreed. This is a route comparison, not a representation that the paper satisfies any journal's current submission requirements.
